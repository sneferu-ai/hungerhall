# HUNGERHALL — G4 Art Direction Bible: WHITE-GLOVED HUNGER (2D pixel, Godot + Steam)

## Mood declaration

This game looks like **white-gloved** **famished**. It derives from G1's locked 1985 arcade pixel-art contract — 32px tile grid, period-appropriate palette discipline, flat fills with no contemporary lighting — and the published visual principles of *Gauntlet* (Atari, 1985: cabinet palette economy, four-class silhouette readability in crowded rooms), *The Legend of Zelda* (Nintendo, 1986: prop legibility inside tiny tile budgets), and *Berzerk* (1980: silhouette-first threat identification under flat lighting). It is NOT the hi-bit bullet-hell jewelry of *Enter the Gungeon*, NOT the painterly prestige-dungeon rendering of *Hades*, NOT scanline-overlay CRT cosplay that mistakes filter noise for period discipline.

**Visual thesis:** The player recognizes this game because the dungeon is laid as a dinner service where the hero's fading health is the only light: every interactive object carries a single cloche crescent of amber warmth that marks what the hall is willing to price in hit points, and the world itself dims in three grades as HP falls — lit amber for what sustains you, dark recess for what consumes you, and a guttering flame on the hero that shrinks as the meal progresses. The enemies are kitchen staff gone feral — cleaver-wielding brutes, boiling cauldrons, pantry porters with tureens — not borrowed bestiary recolored in kitchen tones.

## Three candidate palettes

Three full token systems compete inside this one direction. They share the silhouette grammar, the cloche crescent law, and the three-grade life-light dimming; they argue about which hue carries the service signal and how saturated the world should be.

## Shape language

- **Mark-making:** chunky pixels on a 2×2 atomic unit for fills and marks; 1px strokes permitted for outlines and contact shadows only. Anti-aliasing, fractional placement, and rotated sprites are forbidden. The smallest authored fill is 2×2px; the smallest stroke is 1px.
- **Camera:** fixed top-down orthographic plan view at 640×360 virtual resolution (20×11 tiles at 32px + 8px bottom strip), integer 2× to 1280×720 and integer 3× to 1920×1080. Zero tilt, zero rotation, no parallax, no zoom. Dead-zone follow with 16px lead toward travel direction and 2px trauma kick on generator burst per G2 §11.
- **Silhouette grammar:** threat identity is mass-and-posture before any hue. Scullion Wisp = ragged dishcloth wisp under 20px with trailing soapy film. Cleaver Brute = square slab in butcher's apron, no neck, cleaver arm. Broiler = tall boiling-cauldron triangle with steam vents. Porter = hunched server balancing a tureen overhead. Maître d' = angular vested diamond with detached serving-gloved hands. Carver = 64px hulk crowned by a hollow serving dome with a carving knife. G2's contour gate (≥90% silhouette separation per zone, per threat pair) is the acceptance test this grammar serves.
- **Hero geometry:** equipment on a fixed front stance — square pot helm plus broad cleaver-axe (Warrior), round serving-shield rim plus spear (Valkyrie), wide-brim spice-hat triangle plus spark hand (Wizard), slim hood plus carving-bow nock (Elf). Class color tokens bind to those equipment accents only; silhouette is the primary class identifier, so the standing monochrome-equipment fallback needs no new art. Aim direction is carried by the projectile and its muzzle flash, not by body rotation; the sprite never turns. Facing authored S/E/N; W is a code mirror of E; diagonals reuse the dominant-axis facing — the authentic 1985 cheat.
- **Outlines:** heroes, enemies, and interactive objects (generators, food, keys, potions, chests, closed door hatches) carry a 2px canvas outline; the exit hatch carries a spectral_cyan glow rim instead of an outline. Tiles carry no outline — separation comes from value step alone. Scullion Wisps use a 1px text_bone outline to signal ethereal wall-passing. The 1px canvas contact slit under every actor is a code-drawn rectangle at the sprite's origin, never part of any Forge-rendered sprite asset.
- **Materials:** flat matte mineral and kitchen-surface textures everywhere. A wet or hot sheen is permitted only on interactive seams — cloche slot, key teeth, chest lid, void-tile edges — and never exceeds 2px. No gradients, no specular highlights, no baked cast shadows.
- **Lighting — the life-light system:** three baked world-value grades (well-fed 100–50% HP ×1.0, hungry 50–25% ×0.80, famished <25% ×0.62) crossfade over 400ms via engine CanvasModulate on GameCanvasLayer (layer 0) only. The UI sits on UICanvasLayer (layer 1) held at ×1.0; captions sit on OverlayCanvasLayer (layer 2) at ×1.0; both are excluded from dimming. Service_amber crescents dim with the world; their relative brightness against the dimmed world remains constant (linear modulation preserves ratios), not increasing — readability is preserved by hue contrast between amber and the plum-stone palette, not by relative luminance. The hero flame overlay frame-swaps per grade (no sprite scaling — see Asset list). Reduced-motion pins grades with no crossfade per G2 §8.4. Warmth (service_amber) is rationed to interactive objects and UI focus; spectral_cyan is reserved for ghost and void phenomena. No directional normals, no rim lights, no real-time shadows. - **Signature motif — PRIMARY: the cloche crescent.** Exactly one lit amber slot where a lid meets its seat, appearing on the generator dome, door and exit hatches, chest lids, potion stoppers, key bows, and the corner cut of destructive UI plaques. One per object; a second crescent is decoration and fails review. At 32px the crescent is minimum 4×2px (8×4px physical at 2× scale) and reads as a lid-seat mark; at 16px (pickups) it is authored as a 2×2px service_amber dot, which reads as a warm glint. If the accessibility tool receipt shows the dot failing to register as an interactive marker, the fallback is a 2×2px solid amber square with no crescent implication. At capsule thumbnail scale a single crescent is not claimed to read; the aggregate multi-crescent scene composition carries capsule identity (see Storefront). The cloche crescent is the sole primary identity marker. SECONDARY — an HP-system detail, not a co-equal motif: the hero flame, a 3-frame sprite (6×6, 4×4, 2×2 sub-regions within a 16×16 frame) on the hero's head, tied to HP grade via RegionRect frame swap with no sprite scaling. At famished grade the crescent carries interactive readability by ubiquity (present on every interactive object) while the flame carries HP readability by personal scale (present only on the hero); they operate at different scales and never compete.
- **Refused defaults:** no post-process bloom, no screen-space glow fields, no per-pixel dynamic lighting, no real-time shadows, no normal maps, no anti-aliased rescaling, no gradient fills, no dithered gradients, no floating dust motes, no ornamental fantasy filigree, no hue-only status coding, no health bars above enemies, no floating damage numbers on non-lethal hits, no parallax, no sprite rotation. Local additive blending on particle sprites is a sprite-level blend mode, not a glow field, and remains permitted.

**Read ladder (binds every important asset):** at one second — hero class silhouette and flame against the nearest threat's silhouette on the floor value, the falling HP numeral as the brightest pixel on screen, and whether the room reads well-fed or famished. At three seconds — the room's structure, the cloche crescents that separate sustain from consume, the threat ladder sorting by mass and motion (kitchen-staff silhouettes: apron slab, cauldron triangle, tureen-carrier, vested diamond), generator spawn breathing from its slot flare, hazard telegraph states. At ten seconds — cloche rivets on the generator dome, butcher-block grain and plate-ring stains on floor tiles, tallow drips and ledger scratches on wall caps, the 3-frame flame flicker, and the zone's accent speckle. Contrast and detail are spent strictly in that order; 10s-tier details (rivets, stains, drips, scratches) are craft polish visible at close inspection, not acceptance blockers — they enrich without gating.

## UI style

**Material-and-interaction thesis — "the hall's table service."** Every control is a piece of serving ware the maître d' sets before the player: matte ui_slate charger-plaques with a 2px chiseled text_bone rim, menu columns arranged like a bill of fare, results panels presented as the check. Input is a candle being brought nearer the table — focus lays a 2px service_amber inlay just inside the rim; hover warms it to half strength; press seats the plaque down 1px and floods the inlay for 100ms, the lid settling onto the dish. Destructive or floor-forfeiting actions (Quit, Delete Save, Reset Scores) arrive as the bill: critical_brick rim, an exclamation glyph, and one crescent clipped from the plaque's top-right corner — the motif's only UI appearance, so leaving the table is the one action that looks like a bite taken out of it. The crescent corner cut is a quarter-circle removed from the plaque's top-right corner: radius 4px virtual for 16px body-text buttons (8px physical at 2×), radius 8px virtual for 24px and larger CTA buttons (16px physical at 2×); destructive plaques are minimum 120×32px virtual, so the cut stays distinguishable at every shipping scale. The cut is implemented by a custom `StyleBox` subclass (`ClippedPlaqueStyleBox`) that draws the plaque fill, rim, inlay, and clipped corner in one draw pass; the destructive button's normal/hover/pressed/focus styles are set to `StyleBoxEmpty` so the default rectangular background is never rendered behind it. Secondary surface treatment is bounded to one named role: the translucent caption band (#000000CC behind white type) mandated by G3. Nothing else receives glass, glow, texture, or elevation.

**Argument (116 words):** A style label — flat, pixel, skeuomorphic — would name an axis, not a decision. The table-service system is derived: G3 freezes a hall that speaks through its furniture and reports errors as spills; G2 freezes fixed-ratio honesty, so rewards print exact arithmetic on plaques rather than slot-machine chrome. Against Palette A, bone-on-slate carries hierarchy and the single rationed amber marks interactivity without a third material. The "bill" interaction — a bite-mark corner on destructive actions — makes the service metaphor load-bearing in interaction, not just cosmetic. Hierarchy is value-plus-inlay, not texture, so the system survives the weakest target (1280×720) intact. With the title hidden, notched slate chargers under candle-warm inlay still read as this game's furniture, not genre chrome.

### Player visual + animation states

Each hero class ships as one 6-frame main cycle (1 idle + 2 walk + 2 throw + 1 potion). The idle frame doubles as throw recovery per G2 §11. The potion frame is a single drinking pose; the class-tinted flash overlay is applied in code. Walk cycles are 2-frame bobs at 90ms. Throw is 1-frame anticipation at 50ms + 1-frame release at 33ms. Death is particle-driven, not sprite-based: on death, the hero sprite scales to 0 over 200ms via code tween while 5–8 smoke particles (wall_body-tinted, normal blend, 600ms upward drift) spawn at the hero's position, and the hero flame detaches and floats upward as a fading service_amber spark — the full hero death presentation spans 1200ms per G2 §2. No dedicated death sprite row is commissioned. All four classes share identical frame layout, animation timing, and facing discipline (S/E/N authored, W = code mirror, diagonals reuse dominant axis). Class identity comes from kitchen-hall equipment geometry first — cleaver-axe arc, serving-shield rim, spice-hat triangle, carving-bow line — reinforced by class color tokens on equipment accents only, never carried by color alone.

The hero_flame_overlay is a shared 3-frame sprite (16×16, service_amber) positioned at the hero's head in code. The three frames are sub-regions within the 16×16 canvas: frame 0 = 6×6px flame (well-fed, 100–50% HP), frame 1 = 4×4px flame (hungry, 50–25%), frame 2 = 2×2px flame (famished, <25%). Code selects the frame via RegionRect per HP grade — no sprite scaling, no bilinear blurring. The 3-frame flicker cycles at 120ms per frame. At famished, a 1-frame smoke wisp appears above the flame. The overlay is on the game layer and dims with CanvasModulate like other game-layer sprites. It is an HP-system detail, not a co-equal signature motif — the cloche crescent is the sole primary identity marker.

### Enemy / NPC sprites

Six enemy types, each a separate manifest row with kitchen-service identities. Five share the 32×32 frame; the Carver occupies 64×64 for boss-scale presence. Threat identity is silhouette-first with culinary transformation: Scullion Wisp (ragged dishcloth wisp with soapy film trail), Cleaver Brute (stocky butcher in stained apron with meat cleaver), Broiler (walking boiling cauldron with steam vents), Porter (hunched pantry server carrying a tureen overhead), Maître d' (angular vested diamond with detached serving-gloved hands), Carver (64px hulk with hollow serving-dome head and carving knife). All carry 2px canvas outlines except the Scullion Wisp, which uses a 1px text_bone outline to signal wall-passing ethereality. The Cleaver Brute uses a wall_cap body with canvas-dark apron plates: wall_cap is lighter than both floor_mid and wall_body, so the slab reads against every surface it crosses. The Carver uses a wall_body body (#191526) with a 2px canvas (#0B0A13) outline — the outline is darker than the body, ensuring the silhouette is visible against any background — and its hollow serving-dome head is rendered in wall_cap (#5A4E76), giving the dome a tonal step against the body that survives even where the outline is obscured. Enemy death is particle-driven: the sprite disappears and 3–5 zone-tinted smoke particles spawn (normal blend, 400ms drift). The particle_smoke texture is wall_body-tinted at render; code sets self_modulate to a 70% blend toward the current zone accent color at spawn. The Carver spawns 8–12 sparks and 6 smoke wisps over 800ms. The generator is an iron serving cloche with a 3-frame cycle (closed 2000ms, open-spawning 150ms, suppressed 500ms) — its crescent slot glows service_amber when spawning and goes dark when suppressed, making the hall's appetite visible as architecture. The generator is categorized as `boundary` for Forge routing because it is a stationary fixture; its visual treatment comes from the hall_fixtures family. **No NPCs.**

### Tile / background art

Four zone floor tiles and four zone wall tiles, each 32×32, fully opaque, organized as two style families. The floor family anchor is tile_floor_drowned; siblings inherit its exact treatment with zone-specific kitchen-surface wear patterns: Drowned Vaults = kitchen tile-floor checkerboard with waterline speckle, Cindercrypt = scorched butcher-block grain with ember seams, Starved Deep = bare pantry flagstones with void dust, Hollow Throne = cracked marble serving counter with fracture veins. The dining-hall metaphor is carried by surface function, not by literal tablecloth or plate imagery on every tile. All wear patterns live in the outer 6px ring, keeping a quiet 20×20 center plane. The wall family anchor is tile_wall_drowned; siblings share the wall_cap top highlight strip (read per zone as grout line, soot line, shelf bracket, or chair rail) and wall_body fill 1.5× darker than floor. Zone accent colors are constrained to ≤15% of tile pixels. No outlines on tiles — separation from actors comes from the value step. Backgrounds are static; no parallax, no animated layers. The manifest commits to the minimum variant plan: one floor tile and one wall tile per zone (8 tile rows total). In-zone variety is delivered at runtime, with no new render rows, by: (1) random 90° rotations and horizontal flips per tile placement; (2) TileMap per-cell TileData.modulate carrying a small seeded value noise (±8% luminance, seeded by tile grid coordinates — never CanvasModulate, which is a global GameCanvasLayer modulator reserved for the life-light system); and (3) code-placed edge/corner decals cut from the existing anchor tile's border strips, rendered as Sprite2D children with self_modulate at 30% zone-accent opacity. This yields up to 64 variant combinations per zone. If the render budget forces further cuts, fall back to 1 floor + 1 wall anchor total and lean entirely on the same runtime variation with zone tints. Two further hazard types (void_bridge, collapsing_void) are deliberately not manifest rows; their visual effects are code-driven using existing floor tiles plus CanvasModulate pulses and code-drawn crack overlays. Only the oven burner flame jet requires a dedicated sprite due to its multi-frame animated cone.

### Particle / effect specs

Four particle textures at 64×64, each a transparent sprite with stepped luminance blocks at 2×2 pixel granularity (not continuous soft falloff): spark (service_amber, additive blend, 4-step tight radial), glow (food_gold, additive blend, 3-step wide halo), smoke (wall_body base tint, 3-step, NORMAL blend — not additive; smoke darkens rather than adds light; code sets self_modulate to a 70% blend toward the zone accent at spawn for enemy deaths), trail (multi-tint via code, additive blend, 4-step elongated streak). G9 implements the emitters in Godot using GPUParticles2D with a ParticleProcessMaterial-style motion contract; additive particles use the canvas_item `add` blend mode and smoke uses `mix`. Example emitter behaviors: on-generator-burst — 8 sparks, service_amber, additive blend, 200ms fade, 2px outward velocity; on-food-pickup — 6 glows, food_gold, additive blend, 400ms rise and fade; on-death-crumble — 5±2 smoke wisps, wall_body-tinted with zone self_modulate, normal blend, 600ms upward drift with gravity 0.5; projectile trail — 1 trail per 12px travel, class-tinted, additive blend, 150ms fade. All timing values are in milliseconds. Additive blending is a sprite-level blend mode, not a post-process glow field; the refused default "no post-process bloom, no screen-space glow" remains intact.

### Alternative direction — synthesis evidence

**Dissenting direction: LIFE-WICK SERVICE** (from draft deepseek-2, avg score 86.1 — second-highest across both scorers).

The dissenting direction centered the hero's health as a candle and made every interactive object carry a lit or unlit wick-mark — lit in amber on what sustains you (food, key, treasure, potion, open door) and unlit in canvas-dark on what consumes you (generator, hazard, locked door). The hero carried the only live flame (3px, 3-frame flicker), and the world dimmed in three grades as HP fell. Its "Tallow Ledger" palette was warmer and more period-authentic (warm near-black canvas, tallow-darkened stone), and its wick-mark motif was more mechanically tied to the health-drain fantasy than the cloche crescent.

**Why the selected direction wins:** The cloche crescent (from moonshot-3, avg score 87.1 — highest across both scorers with matched 92/92 clarity and 88/88 rigor) is more visually distinctive and ties more directly to G3's frozen kitchen-service voice (CHEF, OVEN, PLATED, COURSE, GUESTBOOK, BILL). The wick-mark is elegant but generic — a candle on a hero could belong to any dungeon crawler; a serving cloche on a generator is unmistakably a kitchen that cooks heroes. The selected direction also offers stronger Steam capsule differentiation: a screenshot of cloche-crowned generators in a plum-stone dungeon is instantly recognizable, while a screenshot of a candle-bearing hero in a warm dungeon reads as genre-default.

**What the selected direction adopts from the dissent:** The three-grade life-light dimming system is incorporated wholesale — the world literally darkens as HP falls, the hero's flame shrinks from 6×6 to 2×2 via RegionRect frame swap, and reduced-motion pins the grades without crossfade. This mechanical-visual connection was the dissent's strongest innovation and it enriches the selected direction without diluting the cloche crescent's identity. The warm-temperature argument from Tallow Ledger is preserved as Palette B (Venison Service), now redesigned with a chartreuse hue axis to be a genuine load-bearing alternative rather than a temperature variant.

**Design rationale — scorer disagreement resolution:** The two scorers diverged most sharply on moonshot-2 (zhipu avg 90 vs deepseek avg 79.7, a 10.3-point gap) and on the deepseek self-draft (zhipu avg 86.3 vs deepseek avg 79.8, a 6.5-point gap). For moonshot-2, I sided with deepseek's lower assessment of clarity and rigor — its shape language was 417 tokens vs moonshot-3's 900, and its UI thesis was less derived — but adopted its structural innovation of separate hero class color tokens and zone tint tokens, which zhipu correctly identified as improving palette completeness. For the deepseek self-draft, I sided with zhipu's higher assessment — its CRT Harvest palette had a clean structure and honest accessibility handling — but found the overall direction less distinctive than moonshot-3's kitchen-service commitment. The synthesized artifact takes the strongest structural elements from each draft while committing to a single coherent direction rather than averaging incompatible approaches.
